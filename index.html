07 — A few things worth knowingBefore we talk

Good questions.
Straight answers.

A few answers to help you decide whether Spark is right for your schools.

Ask us something else ↗
Which devices can use Spark?

Spark works in Chrome browsers managed by your school, including Chromebooks. Your technology team installs it through its usual management tools. Tell us what devices you use, and we'll help you check whether they're a good fit.

How does it fit with our current filtering?

Spark works inside your school-managed Chrome browser. It doesn't replace protection for your whole network or apps outside the browser. We can look at your current setup together and discuss where Spark fits.

Can we keep approved sites available?

Yes. Your district can allow approved websites on devices registered with your schools. Those choices apply to your district, without changing the rules for other schools using Spark.

What will we see in district reports?

Devices need to be registered with your district and set up to send reports. A report shows the blocked site's domain name, the device involved, when it happened, and some details about why.

These reports show blocked activity, not a student's full browsing history. Before you get started, we can talk through who can see reports and your district's privacy requirements.

What should we bring to a demo?

An idea of how many devices you have, what you use for filtering now, and a few websites your schools rely on. We'll show you how Spark works and talk through what getting started could look like for your team.
