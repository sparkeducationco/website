Spark Filter is a browser extension from Spark Education Technology for K–12 schools using managed Chrome browsers, including Chromebooks. It helps districts detect proxy bypasses, apply browsing rules, and review blocked activity.

01

Where browser filtering fits

Chromebook web filtering helps schools manage which websites students can access in their managed browser. A district may already use DNS filtering, a network gateway, or another school web filter. Spark adds detection inside Chrome, where browser-based proxies and games run.

That scope matters when evaluating coverage. Spark does not replace protection for your whole network, other browsers, or apps outside Chrome. Review your existing filtering and device policies with your technology team before deciding where it fits.

02

What your technology team can manage

Spark combines configured website rules with detection of supported proxy tools and browser-game activity. District administrators can keep approved websites available and review the reasons for reported blocks.

  • District exceptions: Allow approved sites for devices enrolled with your district.
  • Blocked-activity reports: Review the site, device, time, and detection context for reported blocks.
  • Device enrollment: Check which devices are registered and when they last checked in.

Reporting requires enrolled devices configured to send reports. These reports describe blocked activity; they are not a student's complete browsing history.

03

Plan a managed Chromebook deployment

Start with the people responsible for Chrome management, filtering policy, and student privacy. Confirm which student accounts and devices are managed, how your team deploys extensions, and which policies apply to the pilot group.

Your team deploys Spark through its Chrome management tools and enrolls the pilot devices with the district. Check that the extension is active, the intended policy is applied, and a test block reaches the district reports. An installed extension alone does not establish that reporting is working.

Ask us to walk through your environment in a Spark Filter demo. Bring your device count, current filtering setup, and a list of essential classroom websites.

04

A practical pilot checklist

  • Choose a small, representative group of school-managed Chromebooks.
  • Check learning platforms, assessments, accessibility tools, and other sites your schools rely on.
  • Evaluate supported proxy detection using examples approved by your technology team.
  • Review unexpected blocks and confirm that district exceptions work as intended.
  • Verify device enrollment, policy updates, and blocked-activity reporting.
  • Agree on success criteria and a support process before expanding.

Include everyday classroom use in the evaluation. Detection rules can miss unfamiliar tools or flag legitimate activity, so a pilot should test both filtering behavior and access to learning resources.

05

Review privacy alongside filtering

Browser filtering involves processing browser signals. Before deployment, review the Spark Filter privacy policy, district reporting configuration, access to reports, and your school's requirements for student data.

Ask which information stays on the device, which information is transmitted, and how your deployment is configured. A product description alone does not establish that a school has met its legal or procurement requirements.
